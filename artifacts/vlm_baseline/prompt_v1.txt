TASK: Room cleanliness assessment from a single photograph.

You are assessing ONE photograph of a room or of a kitchen/common area in a shared
residence. Judge only what is visibly present in THIS photograph. Do not guess what
is outside the frame, do not use any other photograph, and do not infer who owns
anything or who caused a condition.

STEP 1 - Look at the whole image first.
Describe to yourself the floor/tiles, the bed or cot area, work surfaces, and any
objects lying around. Then decide.

STEP 2 - Apply this policy.

CLEAN means:
- floor/tiles and visible surfaces are reasonably clean, and
- no meaningful dirt, garbage, waste, debris, spill or residue is visible, and
- any belongings present are reasonably arranged or contained, and
- the room overall looks reasonably neat.
Belongings being present is NOT a problem. Clothes, bags, shoes, bedding, laptops,
chargers, books, bottles, luggage and similar items can all appear in a CLEAN room.
Minor or localized untidiness - for example a few clothes on the cot in an
otherwise neat room - is still CLEAN.
Clothes drying on a stand, normal kitchen items, vessels in use, a dustbin in its
place and storage racks are normal household use, not cleanliness problems.

NOT_CLEAN means at least one of:
- actual dirt, garbage, waste, debris, spill or residue is visible, or the floor or
  a surface is visibly dirty, or kitchen surfaces/vessels are visibly dirty, OR
- objects are scattered or unorganized to the point that the room as a whole looks
  untidy and would reasonably need attention.

UNCERTAIN means the photograph does not show enough to decide - for example it
shows mainly a wall, a ceiling, a window, a curtain or a door, with little or no
floor, furniture or living area visible.

NEVER treat these as cleanliness problems on their own:
- damp patches, peeling paint, wall holes, cracks, stains in the plaster, exposed
  wiring or any other building/maintenance defect.
Mention them only in the maintenance note field.

Do not mark NOT_CLEAN merely because belongings are visible.
Do not mark CLEAN merely because the floor is clean, if the room as a whole is
visibly untidy.

STEP 3 - If and only if the label is NOT_CLEAN, choose the visible reason:
- ITEMS_NOT_PROPERLY_ARRANGED : objects are scattered/unorganized enough to make
  the room look untidy, and no actual dirt/waste/debris is visible.
- VISIBLE_DIRT_WASTE_DEBRIS   : actual dirt, garbage, waste, debris, spill/residue,
  a dirty floor or dirty kitchen surfaces/vessels are visible.
- BOTH                        : both of the above are visibly present.
- NO_SPECIFIC_VISIBLE_ISSUE   : use only if you cannot point to either.
For CLEAN or UNCERTAIN, set predicted_reason to NO_SPECIFIC_VISIBLE_ISSUE.

STEP 4 - Write the evidence.
One or two short sentences naming only what you can actually see in this image.
Name objects and their state, for example "several garments and a bag lie across
the floor beside the bed" or "the counter has food residue and unwashed vessels".
Do not write about anything you cannot see. Do not mention ownership. Do not use
the word "tenant". Do not refer to room numbers, file names or previous labels.

If the image is a kitchen or common area, assess only the visible kitchen/common
area itself.

OUTPUT - reply with ONE JSON object and nothing else. No markdown, no code fence:

{"predicted_label": "CLEAN" | "NOT_CLEAN" | "UNCERTAIN",
 "predicted_reason": "ITEMS_NOT_PROPERLY_ARRANGED" | "VISIBLE_DIRT_WASTE_DEBRIS" | "BOTH" | "NO_SPECIFIC_VISIBLE_ISSUE",
 "evidence_reason": "<one or two short image-grounded sentences>",
 "maintenance_note": "<visible building defects, or empty string>",
 "uncertainty_flag": true | false}

Set uncertainty_flag to true when the view is too limited to judge confidently,
whether or not you chose UNCERTAIN.
